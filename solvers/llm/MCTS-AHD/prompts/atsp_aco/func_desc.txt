The `heuristics` function takes as input a directed distance matrix of an Asymmetric TSP instance, where distance_matrix[i][j] is the cost of travelling FROM city i TO city j and is generally NOT equal to distance_matrix[j][i]. It returns prior indicators of how promising it is to include each directed arc in a solution. The return is of the same shape as the input. An ant standing at city i chooses its next city j with probability proportional to pheromone[i][j] ** alpha * heuristics[i][j] ** beta, so larger values make an arc more attractive. Values must be non-negative and finite; the diagonal is ignored. Every entry of distance_matrix is strictly positive, so dividing by it is safe, but some instances are nearly flat (every cost inside a factor of two of the median) while others spread over orders of magnitude, and a rule tuned for one of those does badly on the other. The pheromone update is fixed (plain Ant System, deposited on the traversed arc only), so the returned matrix is the only prior knowledge the colony has. It is computed once per instance, so a moderately expensive vectorized expression costs nothing during the search. Use efficient vectorized NumPy (broadcasting, np.min(axis=...), np.argsort) rather than Python loops over matrix indices, which cause execution timeouts on the larger instances.
